Tiling Over Plywood

A practical guide to BS 5385 and preparing plywood surfaces for tiling.

Plywood is commonly used in timber floor construction, but it behaves differently from more stable surfaces such as concrete. Changes in moisture and temperature can cause timber-based materials to expand and contract. If the floor isn't properly prepared, this movement can lead to cracked tiles, damaged grout or tiles becoming detached.

BS 5385 covers the design and installation of wall and floor tiling. Its recommendations differ depending on whether you're tiling a wall or a floor. Here's what you need to know when preparing plywood for tiling.

1. Why Plywood Needs Special Consideration When Tiling

Plywood is made from layers of timber veneer bonded together. Although this construction makes it relatively stable, it can still move as moisture levels and temperatures change.

Tiles and grout are much less tolerant of movement. If the plywood flexes or moves beneath the tiled surface, stresses can transfer through the adhesive and into the tiles and grout.

This is why the condition of the underlying structure is so important. Simply covering plywood with tile adhesive and tiles isn't enough to guarantee a reliable installation.

2. What Does BS 5385 Say About Tiling onto Plywood Walls?

BS 5385-1:2018 covers the design and installation of internal wall tiling.

Under this standard, plywood and other wood-based sheets or boards should not be used as direct backgrounds for tiling. Their movement in response to changes in moisture content makes them unsuitable for direct fixing.

If you're tiling a plywood wall, an appropriate tile backer board should be installed first. This provides a suitable surface for fixing the tiles.

3. What Does BS 5385 Say About Tiling onto Plywood Floors?

The guidance for floors is covered by BS 5385-3:2024.

The updated standard recommends using an intermediate layer when tiling over plywood and other wood-based sheets or boards.

The Tile Association identifies three possible options:

  • Tile backer board: A board designed to provide a suitable surface for fixing tiles.
  • Uncoupling membrane: A membrane designed to accommodate differential movement between the timber substrate and tiled surface.
  • Reinforced tanking system: A suitable system that provides an intermediate layer as part of the floor preparation.
  • Whichever system is selected, the underlying floor must still be sufficiently rigid and stable to support the additional weight of the tiling.

Before starting, check:

  • Condition: The plywood should be sound, dry and securely fixed.
  • Fixings: Loose or poorly fixed sheets need addressing before tiling.
  • Movement: The floor should not have excessive bounce or deflection.
  • Moisture: Any underlying damp or moisture problems should be investigated and resolved.

4. How Should You Prepare a Plywood Floor for Tiling?

Once the existing floor has been assessed and is suitable for tiling, an appropriate intermediate layer can be installed.

Two commonly used options are cement-based tile backer boards and uncoupling membranes.

Tile Backer Boards

Cement-based tile backer boards, such as HardieBacker, are commonly used when preparing timber floors for tiling.

They are installed over the plywood using tile adhesive and mechanical fixings, following the manufacturer's instructions. The joints are then treated as specified by the board manufacturer before tiling.

A 6mm board is a popular option for overboarding suitable timber floors, adding very little to the overall floor height. Thicker boards are also available.

The board must be suitable for the intended floor installation. It provides a suitable surface for fixing tiles but will not strengthen a floor that has excessive movement.

Uncoupling Membranes

Uncoupling membranes, such as Schlüter-DITRA, work differently from tile backer boards.

They are designed to accommodate differential movement between the timber substrate and tiled surface, helping to reduce the risk of stress transferring to the tiles.

DITRA is also waterproof. However, if it's being used as part of a waterproofing system, the joints between sheets and connections to walls and other structural elements must be sealed using the appropriate Schlüter accessories.

Both tile backer boards and uncoupling membranes can be suitable options when installed correctly and in accordance with their manufacturer's instructions.

I generally prefer using a cement-based tile backer board, such as 6mm HardieBacker, when preparing suitable timber floors for tiling. However, an uncoupling membrane such as DITRA is also an option, depending on the floor construction and requirements of the installation.

Choosing the Right Tile Adhesive

The adhesive needs to be suitable for the substrate and the products being installed.

When fixing tiles onto a backer board or uncoupling membrane, use a compatible cement-based tile adhesive. A flexible C2TE S1 adhesive is commonly used for these types of installations, but the specific product should always be suitable for the board or membrane and the tiles being fitted.

Where an uncoupling membrane is used, the adhesive beneath the membrane must also be compatible with both the underlying plywood and the membrane's fleece backing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you tile directly onto plywood?

For walls, BS 5385-1:2018 does not recommend direct tiling onto plywood. For floors, BS 5385-3:2024 recommends an intermediate layer, such as a tile backer board, uncoupling membrane or reinforced tanking system.

Do you need to use cement board over plywood before tiling?

No. A cement-based tile backer board is one option, but it isn't the only one. An appropriate uncoupling membrane or reinforced tanking system can also be used, depending on the installation.

Can you use an uncoupling membrane over plywood?

Yes. Uncoupling membranes such as Schlüter-DITRA are one of the intermediate layer options identified in the guidance for plywood floors. The plywood and supporting floor structure must still be suitable, and the membrane must be installed according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Why can tiles crack when laid over plywood?

The most common cause is movement in the floor beneath the tiles. If the plywood or supporting timber structure flexes, that movement can transfer to the tiled surface, causing tiles or grout to crack or become detached. Correct preparation and a suitable intermediate layer help reduce this risk.
